# MMD import

PMX 2.0/2.1 and PMD 1.0 models can be selected as custom characters. VMD motions and VPD poses can also be added directly and retargeted onto s&box Human, Citizen or another humanoid armature without an MMD model. The importer supplies a built-in standard MMD source skeleton, including fingers, common groove/twist controls and foot/toe IK. FK-only poses keep their authored joint rotations.

For exact source proportions, custom bones and model physics, optionally add the character's PMX/PMD or use the skeleton button on the motion row. A single matching model beside the motion is selected automatically; otherwise the standard skeleton is used. An explicitly selected model always takes precedence. The mapping report identifies when the standard source skeleton is used.

Code conversions enable `RetargetRequest.UseStandardMmdSkeleton` by default. Low-level `Retargeter.ImportSource` retains strict companion validation unless called with `useStandardMmdSkeleton: true`; `MmdImporter.Import` has the equivalent `useStandardSkeleton` argument.

Extract the complete model archive before importing. Keep its texture folders beside the model. Diffuse textures, multiplying `.sph` and additive `.spa` sphere maps, and custom PMX toon textures are copied into the project. BMP/sphere textures are converted to PNG in C# using the editor's image decoder. Missing textures produce an error that names the missing file. Materials use a toon ramp and skinned outline shells; shared toon ramps use a built-in two-tone fallback. Outline thickness is in model space, so it differs from MMD's screen-space outline.

VMD interpolation, Japanese bone names, IK enable keys, CCD IK, append transforms, vertex/group morphs and bone morphs are imported. Facial weights are retained in preview and DMX output. ModelDoc uses stable ASCII morph identifiers because its flex-expression parser does not accept Japanese identifiers. Those identifiers are generated by `DmxWriter.MorphName`; the imported clip retains the original names.

Rigid-body physics is sampled during import by a vendored, managed C# Bullet port. Spheres, boxes, capsules, collision masks, damping, friction, restitution and six-axis spring joints are read from the model. Kinematic bodies follow the motion; dynamic bodies drive bones; rotation-only bodies retain animated bone positions. The simulation uses MMD's downward gravity and a ground plane, with steps no larger than 1/120 second. Physics becomes ordinary bone animation in the output. Hair/skirt/helper animation is retained when converting onto a compatible custom MMD rig with matching bone names and hierarchy. A target without those bones cannot reproduce their motion.

PMX impulse morphs feed local or world-space impulses into the managed Bullet simulation, including impulses reached through group morphs. Their effects become ordinary bone animation.

## Advanced playback

PMX/PMD custom targets use stable ASCII bone identifiers in compiled assets, retaining the original Japanese names for motion matching. If a target was loaded before this fix and reports a non-anatomical bind pose, remove it and select the original PMX/PMD again to rebuild its preview and mapping.

Converting onto a PMX/PMD custom target also writes one `.mmdanim` resource per clip. The conversion report lists each resource. Add **MMD Playback** to a GameObject, select its **Animation** resource, and use **Playing**, **Speed** and **Position** to play or seek. The component renders the character itself, so it does not require a separate ModelRenderer. It also displays in the scene editor.

The resource contains the original PMX/PMD definitions, the solved bone transforms, morph controls, material references and baked soft-body vertices. The C# renderer evaluates SDEF spherical deformation and QDEF dual-quaternion skinning, vertex/group morphs, UV and additional-UV morphs, additive/multiplicative material morphs and sphere subtexture coordinates. Bone morphs and rigid physics have already been sampled into the solved poses. The standard VMDL remains available for ordinary skeleton animation workflows; it cannot reproduce these additional rendering features.

Cloth and rope use an adapted C# position-based solver with structural/bending links, animated pins, rigid-body anchors, gravity, damping, friction and sphere/box/capsule collisions. Vertex samples are baked during export, so seeking does not restart a live simulation. This is an approximation rather than Bullet's complete soft-body implementation. The component uses reusable CPU mesh buffers; large meshes and many simultaneous characters cost more than ordinary GPU-skinned models.

Everything runs inside the retargeter. No Blender, Python, MMD executable, native Bullet DLL or external bake process is required.

Current limits:

- Without a source model, the standard rig approximates proportions and cannot reconstruct arbitrary custom controllers, bone morph definitions or hair/clothing physics. Unrecognized tracks are reported and skipped. Camera/light-only motion cannot animate a humanoid; a custom-only motion needs its matching source model.

- Standard VMDL and the retargeter's skeletal preview use linear skinning and position morphs. Use the exported MMD Playback component for SDEF/QDEF, UV/material changes and soft-body deformation.
- Soft-body cluster solving, pressure/volume preservation, aerodynamics, soft-to-soft collisions and two-way soft-to-rigid coupling are not implemented. Those original parameters remain in the resource; the current cloth/rope approximation does not apply them. Bending graph distance is capped at 16 and positional iterations at 128.
- Non-spring PMX 2.1 joint types are not simulated. Sphere mode 3 uses the first additional UV channel; the other additional channels are preserved and morphed but are not consumed by the built-in MMD material.
- Camera, light and shadow VMD tracks are not character animation and are ignored.
- BulletXNA is based on Bullet 2.79; results are not guaranteed to match MMD's solver version or simulation settings exactly. The timeline starts from the first sampled pose, without a hidden warm-up.
- Morph names and extra bones must exist on the target. Citizen does not acquire MMD facial shapes or hair bones from the motion.
- Models are limited to 4,096 bones and 1,024 rigid bodies; sampled clips are limited to eight million bone-frames. Soft-body export is capped at sixteen million vertex-frames. Malformed data reports an error.

The source mesh and materials are converted to ordinary project assets. See [third-party notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) for the managed Bullet port and its local adaptations.
